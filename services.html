Search & Rescue Lifecycle

How a SAR Mission Unfolds

A missing person incident is an urgent, precision-managed operation from first notification to safe extraction.

  1. 1. Activation by Law Enforcement

    Searches are initiated when family or friends report a missing person to 911. Police agencies (Fredericton Police Force or RCMP) assess the scenario and officially activate YSSR via group pager and automated emergency notification.

  2. 2. Incident Command & Strategic Planning

    Our Search Commanders and Overhead Management Team deploy on scene, establishing a mobile command post. Using lost-person behavioral statistics, topographic mapping, and weather forecasts, they calculate high-probability search segments.

  3. 3. Tactical Ground Search

    Trained searchers deploy into the field in coordinated teams. We utilize hasty trails searches, sign-cutting tracking, canine support, night thermal optics, and tight grid sweeps through dense woods, wetlands, rivers, and cliffs.

  4. 4. Medical Stabilization & Extraction

    Once the subject is located, our first-aid trained personnel provide immediate medical stabilization, hypothermia treatment, warmth, and hydration before coordinating gentle stretcher evacuation or air-rescue extraction.

Apparatus & Logistics

Rescue 11: Mobile Command Unit

Our newest response vehicle, Rescue 11, serves as a self-contained operational command post and field communications hub in remote areas of New Brunswick.

Equipped with independent power generation, high-gain VHF repeating radios, satellite internet connectivity, computerized GIS mapping stations, and emergency scene floodlighting, Rescue 11 allows our search coordinators to direct field teams under extreme weather conditions.

Vehicle Capabilities:

All-weather 4x4 response • Onboard GIS workstations • Patient warming & hypothermia gear • UAV drone battery recharging • Dual-band VHF field communications.

Equipment That Saves Lives

Our inventory includes GPS units, wilderness tracking sticks, specialized Stokes basket stretchers, AED defibrillators, hypothermia kits, and high-intensity thermal search lights.

Prevention & Public Safety

AdventureSmart Education Programs

The best search and rescue mission is the one that never has to happen. YSSR volunteers deliver free AdventureSmart workshops across local schools and community organizations.

Hug-a-Tree and Survive

Designed for children in Kindergarten through Grade 5. Teaches kids what to do if they get lost in the woods, the importance of staying put by a tree, staying warm, and how to help searchers find them.

Request school presentation →

Survive Outside

Aimed at teens, adults, and backcountry recreationists. Covers the critical Three Ts: Trip Planning, Training, and Taking the Essentials (The 10 Essentials Checklist) to survive unexpected overnights.

Book a community session →

PaddleSmart & Snow Safety

Water and winter presentations for canoeists, kayakers, paddleboarders, skiers, and snowmobilers covering cold shock, moving water hazards, ice safety, and hypothermia response.

Community Partnership

Beyond The Woods: Civic Support

YSSR gives back to Fredericton and surrounding towns through year-round civic service.

Fredericton Public Safety & Events

Whenever our community gathers, YSSR volunteers step up. Our members provide crowd management, first aid stations, communications support, and logistical marshaling for premier regional events:

  • Fredericton Marathon: Course safety marshaling and emergency communication along race routes.
  • Harvest Music Festival: Assisting municipal emergency services and festival crowds.
  • Spring Freshet Flood Response: Tending road-closure barricades and monitoring rising Wolastoq River levels in coordination with Fredericton EMO.
